7. METHODOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pareto efficiency identifies policies for which no alternative policy is at least as good on both selected objectives and strictly better on one.
A Pareto-efficient policy is not automatically the operationally best policy. Selection requires operational preferences, constraints, and validated cost or capacity information.
The maximum port-specific peak is a temporal overlap indicator derived from port-call intervals. It is not verified berth occupancy or physical port capacity.
The recovery rules are hypothetical assumptions. The observed inter-port elapsed times are not pure sailing times and do not establish actual recoverable schedule slack.
The underlying call sequences do not independently confirm Strait of Hormuz transits. No actual route or AIS crossing is inferred by this analysis.

8. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Only three recovery policies are compared.
- Only two objectives are used for Pareto analysis.
- Port concurrency is a proxy, not a capacity measure.
- No verified AIS trajectories or Strait crossings are used.
- Recovery fractions and fixed recovery hours are hypothetical.
- The equal-rank sum is descriptive and not a validated multi-objective optimisation function.
